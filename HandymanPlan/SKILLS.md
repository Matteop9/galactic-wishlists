# The Pre-Trip Skills Curriculum 🎓

Everything you two can learn **free, online, before you go** — and keep learning on the road. This is the printable/readable version of the Skills tab in [index.html](index.html), with fuller notes. All resources below were verified free (YouTube, free-to-audit courses, or free knowledge bases) as of August 2026.

**How to level up:** for each skill, both of you track: *Not started → Learning (watched/read) → Practiced (done it once on something low-stakes) → Confident (would do it on a house we're selling)*. Watching videos gets you to Learning. Only tools in hands gets you further — practice on your own place, family projects, or the first (cheapest) property.

**The golden rule of what to DIY:** finishes and labour-heavy work = yours. Certified systems = never yours. In most of Europe, electrical, gas and often septic work must be done or certified by registered professionals for the house to be **sellable, insurable and connectable** — an uncertified DIY rewire is a liability, not an asset. Learn these systems to *understand, budget and supervise*, not to sign off.

## ⚠️ Continental Europe ≠ UK/US YouTube

Most DIY content assumes timber studs, plasterboard and cavity walls. The houses you'll buy are different:

1. **Solid masonry everywhere.** Stone rubble, solid brick or hollow clay block — no cavity, no stud bays. Insulation goes inside (insulated linings) or outside (EWI + render, standard on the continent). Everything you hang goes into masonry with plugs and resin anchors.
2. **Cables & pipes are chased into walls** in conduit (*saignée* / *rozas*), then plastered over — see the Leroy Merlin video. Budget double the time and dust of UK/US methods for any services alteration.
3. **Electrics are radial** (no UK ring mains), Schuko/French sockets, and every country has its own certification regime. Understand; don't sign off.
4. **Lime vs gypsum is THE materials distinction.** Pre-war masonry was built with lime and must breathe. Gypsum skim, cement render or plastic paint on it traps moisture and creates the blown plaster and damp these ruins are full of. Mike Wye + SPAB + Heritage House are your lime canon; Haduck and Giordano's masonry-first US methods are closer to continental practice than most UK content.
5. **Windows open inward** as monobloc units, often with integrated roller shutters; external timber shutters need routine repair/repaint. Measure and buy locally.
6. **Old roofs** are commonly low-pitch terracotta with no membrane — slipped tiles and wind-blown rain matter more. Assess hard before buying.
7. **Septic is normal.** Learn the anatomy, read inspection reports, budget €3.5–12k for a failed one.

## Suggested pre-trip order (evenings & weekends, ~3 months)

1. **Both, first:** Phase 2 (Assess & Survey) — it decides what you buy, and it's all watchable.
2. **Split and specialise:** one of you leads decorating + tiling + silicone (Phase 3 finishing), the other leads plumbing + heating + septic awareness (Phase 4). Overlap on kitchens and flooring.
3. **Practice for real before you go:** repaint a room properly, re-silicone a bathroom, lay a laminate floor for family, fit a flat-pack kitchen for anyone who'll let you. Tool time beats screen time 10:1.
4. **Language:** 15 min/day Duolingo + Language Transfer on drives, focused on your first target country.
5. **On the road:** Phases 5–6 skills slot in per-project; lime work is best learned by doing a small patch on your own first house.
