# The Europe Live-In Flip Playbook 📖

Strategy notes for the buy → live in → renovate → sell journey. Compiled from a research sweep of country guides, tax/legal sources, renovation cost guides and accounts of people who've done it (August 2026). Figures are planning ranges, not quotes — **always verify locally before an offer, and get country-specific tax advice before the first purchase.** None of this is legal, tax or investment advice.

---

## 1. The strategy, honestly assessed

What you're describing is a **live-in flip**, and it's a real, proven strategy. Its two superpowers:

- **No forced exit.** You need somewhere to live anyway, so slow rural markets and 2× timelines are survivable where they'd kill a financed flip. Track *incremental* holding cost vs renting, not gross.
- **Labour arbitrage.** At the €10–100k end, renovation labour is most of the cost. Skilled DIY on finishes (see [SKILLS.md](SKILLS.md)) can cut 30–50% off mid-tier renovation costs.

Its three structural problems (all solvable, none optional):

1. **You can't legally live in Schengen long enough to do it as tourists.** 90 days in any rolling 180 across the *whole* Schengen area — and the EES biometric system (fully operational since April 2026) enforces it strictly. Owning property grants no right to stay. **A national long-stay visa (or Ireland, see below) is a precondition of the whole plan**, and it's also what unlocks main-residence tax reliefs and (in Portugal) resident purchase-tax rates.
2. **European transaction costs are brutal.** Round-trip (buy + sell) costs run 10–18% of price. On a €50k flip that's €5–9k gone before profit — paid on *every* hop. Fewer, bigger, longer flips beat many small fast ones.
3. **Serial flipping can get you reclassified as a professional trader** (income tax + possibly VAT instead of CGT — Greece explicitly triggers at 3+ sales in 2 years; France calls it *marchand de biens*; Ireland has an anti-flip carve-out in its main-residence relief). One flip every 18–36 months with genuine residence looks like a homeowner. Three in three years looks like a business.

**The shape that works:** get residency in one country → buy the ugliest structurally-sound house in the best village you can afford → live in it as your genuine main residence while renovating over 12–30 months → sell → repeat, at a pace that stays on the right side of "homeowner". Jersey has no CGT, but that doesn't shield you — gains are taxed where the property sits, under that country's rules.

## 2. The 90/180 problem & where you can actually stay

| Route | The deal |
|---|---|
| 🇮🇪 **Ireland (Common Travel Area)** | British citizens — *explicitly including Jersey* — can live, work and buy indefinitely. No visa, no clock. Ireland is also **not in Schengen**, so Irish days never touch your 90/180. The natural base/reset country — but property is expensive (cheapest: Leitrim, Longford, Roscommon; derelict cottages €30–90k exist) |
| 🇵🇹 **Portugal D7** | The easiest visa in Europe: ~€920/month passive income (+50% for partner), some savings in a Portuguese account. Being resident also unlocks near-zero IMT on cheap houses and CGT reinvestment relief |
| 🇪🇸 **Spain NLV / DNV** | Non-lucrative: ~€2,400/month passive, no work at all. Digital nomad: ~€2,849/month of remote income for non-Spanish clients — the realistic route if one of you works remotely |
| 🇫🇷 **France VLS-TS visiteur** | ~€1,500–1,800/month per person of means, renewable in-country; renovating your own home is fine |
| 🇮🇹 **Italy** | Weakest option: elective residence needs ~€38k/yr *passive* income (couple); DNV needs ~€28k/yr remote income + degree + history |
| 🇬🇷 **Greece FIP/DNV** | ~€3,500/month — high bar |
| 🇧🇬 **Bulgaria** | Now **fully Schengen (2025) + euro (2026)** — no longer a clock-stopper. Type D visa routes: pensioner, trade rep office, or running your own Bulgarian company |

**Tax residency follows:** 183+ days anywhere = tax resident there (worldwide income, but also main-residence reliefs). This interacts with Jersey status — get one proper cross-border tax consultation before committing. Treat it like a €500 survey on the whole strategy.

## 4. Renovation cost benchmarks (2025–26, trades-led)

DIY labour cuts 30–50% off the middle tiers. Bulgaria runs ⅓–½ of Western prices.

| Job | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Full rewire (80–120m²) | €3–10k (BG €1.5–4k) | Incl. consumer unit + certification; certified pro mandatory nearly everywhere |
| New pitched tile roof | €60–130/m² re-cover; €100–250/m² if timbers gone | **The** big variable — price every purchase assuming timbers are bad until proven otherwise |
| Complete bathroom | €4–12k (BG €2–5k) | Creating one where none exists adds drainage work |
| Complete kitchen | €5–15k; DIY flat-pack route from ~€3k | Prime DIY territory |
| Windows (uPVC, installed) | €250–700/unit; whole house €4.5–12k | Stone-wall openings push labour up |
| Damp correction (whole small house) | €2.5–8k | Ground levels + drainage + lime + ventilation — much of it DIY; avoid injection DPCs in stone |
| Septic replacement | €3.5–12k | France: non-conforming system must be fixed within 1 yr of purchase — negotiate it off the price |
| Structural repair / underpinning | €800–1,500/linear m; €6–40k per wall | If a survey suggests foundation movement: **walk away** |
| Full replumb | €4–10k (BG €2–5k) | PEX first-fix is DIY-friendly |
| Heating | Wood stove €1.5–5.5k · pellet €3–7k · heat pump €10–20k | Stove + electric backup is the standard flip choice |
| Interior plaster | €13–25/m² (lime +30–100%) | Very DIY-learnable |
| Interior painting | €1.5–4k by trade; <€500 DIY materials | Highest-return DIY of all |
| Flooring | €18–70/m² fitted (+€10–30/m² levelling) | Laminate/tile = prime DIY |

**€/m² all-in tiers:** cosmetic refresh €200–450 (BG €100–250) · medium reno €400–900 (BG €250–450) · full structural €700–1,500 (BG €300–800). On a typical 80–120m² purchase, a genuine full renovation costs €25–100k+ in the west, €25–60k in Bulgaria — **often more than the purchase price. That's the central maths of the whole venture.**

## 5. The dirty-dozen surprises (pre-offer checklist)

1. **No damp course anywhere** — normal for pre-1950. If soil/concrete outside sits above interior floor level, damp is guaranteed. Fix with drainage + lime + ventilation, not injections or cement.
2. **Roof timbers rotten under perfect tiles** — probe rafter ends in the loft. Doubles the re-roof price.
3. **Illegal/unregistered extensions** — ~20% of Italian properties; endemic in rural Spain. You cannot resell what a notary won't transfer. Make the SELLER regularise before completion.
4. **No legal utility connection** — rural power connection can be €3k fixed + €110–150/m of line. Binding operator quote before buying.
5. **Failed septic** — see benchmarks; use the inspection report as negotiation leverage.
6. **Asbestos** (fibro-cement roofs on barns, 1950–90) — €25–60/m² licensed removal; DIY removal illegal in most states.
7. **Well/water rights not in the title** — verify legality, flow, potability.
8. **No legal access** — a house without a recorded right of way is nearly worthless. Have the notary confirm it explicitly.
9. **Boundary/cadastre mismatches** — walk the boundaries with seller and neighbours; fix as a condition of sale.
10. **Protected zones** — within 500m of a French monument or under an Italian vincolo, even window colours need approval; imposed materials add 20–50% to those lines.
11. **Quotes excluding VAT** — standard in Greece (24%!). Always ask.
12. **Everything ×2** — renovations run 50–100% over timeline via small compounding delays. Carry 20–30% contingency on old stone and plan life around the pessimistic schedule.

## 6. Deal rules (what the tracker enforces)

- **Work backwards from the ceiling.** Every village has a price above which buyers simply buy somewhere nicer. Get it from the best *sold* renovated comps. Renovation budget = ceiling − everything else − profit. Never renovate past it: match the local standard "one tier better, not three".
- **Max offer, computed not guessed:** `MAO = realistic sale price − reno (incl. 20–30% contingency) − buy costs − sell costs − holding − minimum profit in absolute €`. The US "70% rule" under-discounts for Europe's transaction costs — at these price points compute it explicitly (the Deal tab does).
- **A deal must survive the pessimistic case.** If profit only exists in the optimistic scenario, it's not a flip, it's a hope.
- **Aim for ≥ €1 profit per €1 of renovation spend**, and judge projects on **annualised** ROI (50% over 3 years ≈ 14%/yr) — that's the honest comparison between "flip again here" vs "move country".
- **Keep every invoice.** In Italy/Spain/Portugal documented renovation reduces CGT directly — cash-in-hand work is invisible to the taxman *in the bad direction*. France (uniquely) gives +15% with no invoices after 5 years — the DIYer's friend.
- **Structure beats charm:** buy ugly-but-sound over pretty-but-cracked, every time. At €10–30k, assume the building contributes nothing — you're buying walls, land and permission.

## 7. Living in it without losing your minds

From people who've done it (renovating couples' accounts, forums, a Houzz survey in which >50% of renovating couples reported serious stress and 12% considered divorce):

- **Finish one room first and defend it.** A clean, dust-sealed, renovation-free bedroom/living space before you demolish anything else. Door discipline + taped plastic; the retreat room is sacred.
- **Habitability before beauty:** watertight roof → one working bathroom → heat source → temporary kitchen → *then* pretty things. Get the bathroom and heat done before winter — multiple accounts call an unheated winter the strategy's low point.
- **Agree the money and decision rules before starting:** budget ceiling, who decides what, and an agreed "pause/abort" criterion — the same way you carry financial contingency. ~20% of renovators admit making a major decision without consulting their partner; don't be them.
- **Schedule genuinely renovation-free days** off-site. Dust fatigue and decision fatigue are cumulative.
- **It's a flip, not your forever home.** Every material choice gets tested against comps, not taste. Over-personalising and over-improving are the documented failure modes of live-in flips.
- **Insulate.** EPC ratings move European sale prices (France now effectively penalises F/G stock), and thermal comfort is what you'll personally feel all winter. One couple's most-valued outcome of a whole stone-house renovation: energy class G → A4.

## 8. Suggested first move

A trial run before the full nomad life: **one project, deliberately imperfect conditions.** Two candidate shapes —

- **Ireland first:** no visa clock at all, English-speaking, learn the full buy-renovate-sell cycle end-to-end (accepting thinner margins and 33% CGT), then go continental with a proven playbook; or
- **Portugal first:** D7 visa (the cheapest residency in Western Europe), become tax resident (unlocking resident IMT + reinvestment relief), and do a Guarda/Castelo Branco district house as the pilot.

Either way: cheap house, structurally sound, cosmetic-to-medium scope, 12–18 months, and treat everything you get wrong as tuition. The tracker's Journal tab is for exactly that.
